## Buyer-intent findings

### adhd-adults-workbook-es
- **Persona:** Spanish-speaking adult, roughly 25–45, recently diagnosed or
  suspecting it, often after a child's diagnosis. Holds a job, runs a household,
  and loses the same three objects every week.
- **Job to be done:** get through an ordinary day without losing things,
  forgetting appointments, or starting every morning from zero.
- **Search intent:** "cómo organizarme con TDAH", "rutina mañana TDAH adulto",
  "perder las llaves TDAH", "plantillas organización TDAH".
- **Competing content:** clinic and health publishers with long tip lists
  (INECAP, GAM Medical, Banner Health in Spanish, HelpGuide, El Neuropediatra).
  All authoritative, all general.
- **Gaps we can fill:** one concrete system per article instead of ten tips; a
  non-clinical voice in Spanish, which is rare in this niche; systems that need a
  notebook rather than an app or a printer; and Spanish-specific admin pain
  (papeleo, citas) that English-sourced translations never cover.

### bilingual-english-spanish-kids-vocab
- **Persona:** a parent in a mixed or heritage household — one parent's Spanish is
  the minority language at home — plus bilingual-classroom teachers as a
  secondary audience.
- **Job to be done:** get the child to actually *use* the weaker language daily,
  without a fight and without a worksheet session nobody has time for.
- **Search intent:** "bilingual activities for kids at home", "how to get my child
  to speak Spanish", "English Spanish vocabulary games", "raising bilingual kids
  screen free".
- **Competing content:** a large printable ecosystem (Brightly, Woo! Jr.,
  Studycat, Bilingual Balance, El Recreo Spanish, TPT, Etsy).
- **Gaps we can fill:** almost everything assumes a printer and prep time. Nothing
  covers the emotional moment that actually stalls families — the child who
  answers in the other language — with concrete parent scripts in both languages.

### ai-workflows-accountants-pt
- **Persona:** owner or manager of a small Brazilian accounting office, 2–15
  staff, doing document intake and client questions personally.
- **Job to be done:** take hours out of document handling and client replies
  without buying another system or risking client data.
- **Search intent:** "IA na contabilidade como usar", "prompt ChatGPT
  contabilidade", "automatizar conciliação bancária escritório contábil".
- **Competing content:** vendor blogs. Useful, and every one of them ends in a
  product. One source reports over 60% of Brazilian offices already use some
  fiscal automation while under 15% use AI for tax analysis — the gap between
  curiosity and practice is where our content sits.
- **Gaps we can fill:** vendor-neutral, copy-paste recipes; and the data question
  nobody answers plainly — what must never be pasted into a general AI tool, and
  how to rewrite the request without client data.

## Semantic QA status of the nine drafts

Each draft carries `qa_checklist`, `semantic_review` (what I checked about the
language and register) and `flagged_uncertainty` (what I am *not* sure about).
Flagged items, summarised:

- **Spanish:** regional vocabulary I chose but cannot settle alone — "gestoría"
  (Spain) vs Latin American equivalents, "bol", "a por agua" (Spain only),
  "atascarse". A fluent reader must pick one register; I wrote neutral Spanish but
  will not claim native judgement.
- **Portuguese:** "descaracterizado" vs "sem dados identificáveis"; whether
  "natureza de despesa" is the phrase a Brazilian accountant would use; and two
  sentences about service contracts and signed responsibility that must read as
  office practice, not legal advice.
- **Bilingual (EN with ES scripts):** the kitchen-walk article assumes Spanish is
  the language being built. Half the likely buyers are Spanish-dominant parents
  building English, which needs a mirrored version before we claim the topic is
  covered.

Claims: no medical, financial or educational outcome promises. The ADHD articles
never mention diagnosis or treatment; the accounting articles end in human review
and give no tax guidance; the bilingual articles promise no fluency or school
result.
